# AI task horizon growth

A useful yardstick for frontier progress is how long a stretch of human work the best AI can finish at a stated success rate. That horizon has moved from minutes to hours to weeks over a short span of book cycles, which is the exponential practitioners keep pointing at.[^emollick-ai-task-horizon]

## Synthesis

[Ethan Mollick on AI task horizon growth](../sources/articles/emollick-ai-task-horizon.md) anchors three checkpoints to his own publishing timeline: about four minutes of human work at 50% success when his first AI book came out in 2024; about seventeen hours when he turned in Co-Existence earlier in 2026; and about seventeen weeks when that book published days before the post.[^emollick-ai-task-horizon] He points readers to [30 Months On: The Exponential in Charts](https://open.substack.com/pub/oneusefulthing/p/30-months-on-the-exponential-in-charts) for the fuller chart view.
