The chart of token spend per employee still has no ceiling in sight. And the closer you are to AI right now the deeper the sense is that we’re actually still in the early part of the curve of token use and adoption.

This summer and fall’s model releases have continued to push the state of the art forward at a rate that already felt hard to maintain. As a result, for most productive use-cases the models are only now starting to actually work, and the feeling is that things will only accelerate from here.

Coding, in retrospect, was basically trash even 12 months ago. Now it’s finally starting to get good. And now that coding is actually working, you’re getting tool and computer use, which open up a wide variety of knowledge work domains that weren’t possible to throw agents at before.

Once you have agents that can code, use computers, leverage any tool out there, and understand a variety of domains, you can begin to throw them at a wide variety of knowledge work tasks that were hard to bring automation to across finance, life sciences, cyber, and more. So that brings agents to a broad set of work that we’re not really using them in yet at scale.

Then comes the new form factors of agents. Agent swarms and background agents are going to eat up vastly more tokens than anything we can imagine. You’ll have agents running in most enterprises workflows processing data, validating work, doing research, providing decision support, and so on. And we’ll have swarms that fan out in parallel in areas like cyber, life sciences, coding, and more.

So basically tokens are only going up from here.
