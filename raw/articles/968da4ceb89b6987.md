# harness-evals

[#harness-evals](#harness-evals)

Which AI agent harness should you use with a frontier model? We hold the model (Opus 5.5 or GPT-6.1 Sol) and the tasks fixed, swap only the harness (system prompt, tools, memory), and measure pass rate, cost per solved task and time.

## Claude Code (Opus 5.5)

[#claude-code-opus-55](#claude-code-opus-55)

- Cheap and fast. $0.22 per solved task and 40.1 s per trial, and $0.165 less per trial than OpenClaw on Opus [-$0.206, -$0.128] at the same pass rate in a separate cost breakdown run ([finding](/garrytan/g-harness-evals/blob/main/findings/harness-cost/README.md)).

- A rule in the system prompt or startup file reached every turn. A "check gbrain first" rule was followed in 30 of 30 turns from either, 26 of 30 from a per-turn hook, against 18 of 30 with no rule (descriptive: 10 public tasks, one repetition, command line) ([finding](/garrytan/g-harness-evals/blob/main/findings/rule-placement/README.md)).

- It doesn't open skills on its own. An unmentioned skill was opened 0 times in 30 turns. Adding "Use the brain-first skill." to the prompt got 30 of 30, against 18 for the unmentioned skill and 17 for no rule (descriptive) ([finding](/garrytan/g-harness-evals/blob/main/findings/rule-placement/README.md)).

- Its memory text and tool advice, together, matter on hard tasks. On easy tasks, dropping its memory-and-skills text cut cost per trial 19% ($0.033 [-$0.046, -$0.022]) with no measurable pass change. On harder tasks, dropping it together with its tool-description advice took pass rate from 60.4% to 0.0% (-60.4 points [-79.2, -39.6]); the two were cut together, so the loss can't be pinned on one ([finding](/garrytan/g-harness-evals/blob/main/findings/prompt-cuts/README.md)).

- With gbrain, pass rate recovered once access rules were on. With per-member access rules it passed 50 of 51 (installer) and 49 of 51 (told) against 46 with no brain; with one bot-wide token, 27 and 31. The gains over no brain are descriptive, every interval reaches zero, and gbrain's version also changed between the runs ([finding](/garrytan/g-harness-evals/blob/main/findings/shared-brain-access/README.md)).

## Codex (GPT-6.1 Sol)

[#codex-gpt-61-sol](#codex-gpt-61-sol)

- Lowest point estimate and fastest on Sol (sharing rank 1). $0.17 per solved task and 30.1 s per trial. In the separate cost breakdown run it was $0.023 cheaper per trial than Hermes [-$0.030, -$0.016] and, barely, $0.107 cheaper than OpenClaw [-$0.270, -$0.002] ([finding](/garrytan/g-harness-evals/blob/main/findings/harness-cost/README.md)).

- It follows a rule wherever it sits. 30 of 30 turns from a per-turn hook, the system prompt or the startup file, against 6 of 30 with no rule ([finding](/garrytan/g-harness-evals/blob/main/findings/rule-placement/README.md)).

- An unmentioned skill worked. 30 of 30 turns ([finding](/garrytan/g-harness-evals/blob/main/findings/rule-placement/README.md)).

- Its identity text is a small cut on easy tasks. Removing it (639 bytes per request) cut cost per trial 3% ($0.004 [-$0.007, -$0.001]) with pass unchanged; it is untested on hard tasks ([finding](/garrytan/g-harness-evals/blob/main/findings/prompt-cuts/README.md)).

- With gbrain and access rules, no out-of-scope reads. 0 of 51 trials returned a record the asker may not read; the pass gain over no brain (+12 points [+0, +24] with the installer) is descriptive ([finding](/garrytan/g-harness-evals/blob/main/findings/shared-brain-access/README.md)).

## OpenClaw (Opus 5.5 and GPT-6.1 Sol)

[#openclaw-opus-55-and-gpt-61-sol](#openclaw-opus-55-and-gpt-61-sol)

- About twice the cost on Opus. $0.44 per solved task against $0.19 to $0.24 for the other Opus harnesses, and the slowest at 99.4 s per trial (descriptive, no test) ([finding](/garrytan/g-harness-evals/blob/main/findings/harness-cost/README.md)).

- The money goes to cache writes. About 81% of its extra cost over Claude Code on Opus is cache writes: it re-sends a much bigger system prompt and bigger tool results. On Sol, most of its gap to Codex is output (282,492 output tokens against 32,226), and $0.036 per trial of it is one timed-out stream charged at its full reservation ([finding](/garrytan/g-harness-evals/blob/main/findings/harness-cost/README.md)).

- On Sol, it follows per-turn rules, and an unmentioned skill worked. 30 of 30 turns from a per-turn hook or the startup file (it has no system-prompt setting), and 29 of 30 from an unmentioned skill. A rule given once was followed in 4 of 10 repeat turns (descriptive; the test could only detect a 39-point gap) ([finding](/garrytan/g-harness-evals/blob/main/findings/rule-placement/README.md)).

- Its minimal prompt is a lead, not proof. On Opus it cut $0.023 per trial with pass +7.5 points on easy tasks; the retest on harder tasks hit the floor, so it is untested there ([finding](/garrytan/g-harness-evals/blob/main/findings/prompt-cuts/README.md)).

## Hermes Agent (Opus 5.5 and GPT-6.1 Sol)

[#hermes-agent-opus-55-and-gpt-61-sol](#hermes-agent-opus-55-and-gpt-61-sol)

- Shares rank 1 on cost on both models. $0.24 per solved task on Opus (64.6 s) and $0.19 on Sol (81.5 s) ([finding](/garrytan/g-harness-evals/blob/main/findings/harness-cost/README.md)).

- It sends more per request on Sol. 423 kB more tool schemas per trial and 16.7 requests per trial against Codex's 11.6, which is why it costs $0.023 more per trial than Codex ([finding](/garrytan/g-harness-evals/blob/main/findings/harness-cost/README.md)).

- On Sol, it obeys the hook and the system prompt, not skills. 30 of 30 turns from either; an unmentioned skill did nothing (4 of 30, the same as no rule, opened 0 times). A rule given once was followed in 2 of 10 repeat turns (descriptive; the test could only detect a 39-point gap) ([finding](/garrytan/g-harness-evals/blob/main/findings/rule-placement/README.md)).

- Prompt cuts mostly hit a safety keep. 5 of its 7 cut arms were kept under the safety rule, all resting on one task where Opus read outside the asker's scope in every harness ([finding](/garrytan/g-harness-evals/blob/main/findings/prompt-cuts/README.md)).

## QM (Pi loop, Opus 5.5)

[#qm-pi-loop-opus-55](#qm-pi-loop-opus-55)

- Lowest point estimate for cost per solved task on Opus, sharing rank 1. $0.19 [$0.13, $0.28], tied fastest at 40.1 s ([finding](/garrytan/g-harness-evals/blob/main/findings/harness-cost/README.md)).

- Cheaper per trial than Claude Code. Claude Code cost $0.041 more per trial [+$0.020, +$0.061] ([finding](/garrytan/g-harness-evals/blob/main/findings/harness-cost/README.md)).

- Caveat: QM ran on the default Docker bridge, not the internal network. Its matrix comparisons are untested, and its cost-run contrast with Claude Code did not hold the network fixed ([finding](/garrytan/g-harness-evals/blob/main/findings/harness-cost/README.md)).

## gstack and gbrain

[#gstack-and-gbrain](#gstack-and-gbrain)

- gstack: Opus 5.5 read gstack's skills but didn't follow them. Told to run gstack's /investigate, /qa, /review or /ship, Opus loaded the skill text every time and ran none of its steps: 0 of 46 gstack sessions across three uncounted checks, at about twice the cost per trial of no gstack (dev). The tasks were easy for Opus (every arm passed 14 of 16 dev tasks; the hard check was void), so this shows the steps weren't followed, not whether following them helps. The cost ratio is a point estimate, with no interval ([finding](/garrytan/g-harness-evals/blob/main/findings/2026-11-02-opus-gstack-steps-not-followed.md)).

- gbrain needs per-person access rules. With one bot-wide token, gbrain reads returned records the asker wasn't allowed to see in 33, 20, 17 and 9 of 51 trials; with per-member access rules, 0 of 51 in all four cells ([finding](/garrytan/g-harness-evals/blob/main/findings/shared-brain-access/README.md)).

- Put the "check gbrain first" rule where the agent sees it every turn. On GPT-6.1 Sol, a per-turn hook reached 30 of 30 turns on Codex, Hermes and OpenClaw, and so did the system prompt on Codex and Hermes and the startup file on OpenClaw. Following it cost 10% to 55% more per turn (descriptive) with no pass-rate gain, and with no access rules on the brain, more searches also meant more reads the asker wasn't allowed to see (12 trials) ([finding](/garrytan/g-harness-evals/blob/main/findings/rule-placement/README.md)).
