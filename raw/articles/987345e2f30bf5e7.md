# Anthropic IPO takeaways: ANT debuts with $1.8 trillion market cap in landmark moment for AI

[Ashley Capoot](https://www.cnbc.com/ashley-capoot/)

[Caleigh Keating](https://www.cnbc.com/caleigh-keating/)

[Samantha Subin](https://www.cnbc.com/samantha-subin/)

Anthropic tops $2 trillion market cap in Nasdaq debut: Here’s what experts have to say
[Rapid Recap](https://www.cnbc.com/rapid-recap/)

[Anthropic](/quotes/ANT/) jumped 17% in its Nasdaq debut on Thursday in a seminal moment for artificial intelligence, after the company completed the biggest [initial public offering](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/19/anthropic-ipo-ant-live-updates.html) on record, topping [SpaceX'](/quotes/SPCX/)s historic debut in June. The stock closed at $97.17 per share.

The Claude creator, trading under the ticker "ANT," opened at $111.50, after the company raised $81.3 billion in the IPO. Anthropic sold 980 million shares at $83 a piece on Wednesday, lifting the size and price of its offering.

At Thursday's close, Anthropic had a fully diluted market cap of roughly $1.8 trillion. More than 846 million shares changed hands, surpassing the 522 million shares that were traded on SpaceX's opening day.

The 5-year-old company captivated the corporate world with its Claude AI models and products, which have fueled its meteoric rise this year. Anthropic said in its [prospectus](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/26/anthropic-ipo-live-updates-ai-s-1-filing.html) that it generated $33.24 billion in [revenue](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/02/anthropic-ant-s-1-quarter-financials.html) for the nine months ended Sept. 30, up a staggering 1,200% from the same period last year.

Anthropic CEO[Dario Amodei](https://www.cnbc.com/dario-amodei/) co-founded the company in 2021 after he and a [small group of researchers](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/14/meet-the-seven-co-founders-of-anthropic.html) defected from[OpenAI](https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/OPENAI.FG/), now its chief rival. Anthropic has faced sharp criticism over its[positions](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/19/anthropic-dod-blacklist-court-opening-arguments.html) on safety, regulation and national security, including by President [Donald Trump](https://www.cnbc.com/donald-trump/) and other senior government officials.

CEO and president of Amazon Andy Jassy speaks with CNBC technology correspondent Kate Rooney at the Semafor "Silicon Valley & The World" Conference in San Francisco, California, US, Nov.17, 2026.

## Demonstrators gather outside of Anthropic's offices in San Francisco

Demonstrators against artificial intelligence (AI) during a protest outside of the Anthropic headquarters in San Francisco, California, US, on Thursday, Nov. 19, 2026.
Heather Diehl | Bloomberg | Getty Images

Protestors are gathered outside Anthropic's offices in San Francisco following the company's Nasdaq debut. More than 100 people are there and chanting, "ban AI that builds AI," and passing cars are honking in support.

[Jacob Coxon](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/05/anthropic-openai-google-meta-execs-testify-nyc-council-ai-hearing.html), the former Anthropic researcher who raised early concerns about the potentially dire risks of AI, said he plans to speak at the demonstration, according to [a post on X](https://x.com/hilbertspaess/status/2123148000063377614?a=KoIfVOqlZlUiaN2hBRY&s=20). He shared a link to a website called "Ban RSI," which reads, "Their IPO. Our Lives."

Coxon and other industry researchers have grown worried about [recursive self-improvement](https://www.anthropic.com/institute/recursive-self-improvement), or RSI, which means that an AI system is capable of designing and developing its successor without human intervention. RSI is not yet possible, but companies, including Anthropic, have warned that it would make it easier for humans to lose control over those systems.

## 'Alarmed' Democrats renew calls for AI regulation amid Anthropic IPO, tech outages

Senate Intelligence Committee Vice Chairman Sen. Mark Warner (D-VA) speaks during a news conference on Capitol Hill on June 17, 2026 in Washington, DC.
Andrew Harnik | Getty Images

Investors may be cheering Anthropic's blockbuster trading debut, but some Democratic lawmakers see the mammoth IPO as one more reason to demand regulations on the AI industry.

"A record-breaking IPO for a company worth $1.5 trillion… and still NO action on AI regulation," said Sen. Mark Warner, D-Va., the vice chairman of the Senate Intelligence Committee, in an X post.

"We cannot wait any longer. We must pass my legislation to get some guardrails around this tech," said Warner, referring to [legislation](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/11/19/warner-bipartisan-bill-ai-financing-01175873) unveiled Thursday that would require disclosures from publicly traded companies on AI-related financing.

"I'm alarmed that Anthropic is barreling ahead with its IPO today despite a series of outages in critically important systems," Rep. Pramila Jayapal, D-Wash., said on X.

"Wall Street shouldn't be cashing in on a company that could turn out to be a ticking time bomb or a Trojan horse for hackers and foreign adversaries. Going public cements a broken business model and rewards its reckless pace," Jayapal wrote.

Sen. Chris Van Hollen, D-Md., who is running to be the next Senate majority leader, said on Wednesday, "The last 24 hours and the next 24 hours show us how urgent it is to address the risks posed by AI."

That IPO "will be great for the billionaires, but we need to act urgently to address the threats to the rest of us," Van Hollen said in a [social media video](https://x.com/ChrisVanHollen/status/2123237724236710119?a=ddsSrO0S9aYb124wn-A&s=20).

## Can Anthropic make cyber its next big market? Wolfe Research sees coding-sized opportunity

Samuel Boivin | Nurphoto | Getty Images

Anthropic has made headlines in recent months for its advanced [artificial intelligence](https://www.cnbc.com/ai-effect/) models with sophisticated cybersecurity capabilities.

In a bid to curb bad actors from exploiting them, the AI lab then [limited access](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/07/anthropic-claude-mythos-ai-hackers-cyberattacks.html) to select customers through Project Glasswing.

Anthropic has since [broadened](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/02/anthropic-mythos-ai-project-glasswing.html) the pool of testers and launched a [cybersecurity initiative](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-cyber-mission) aimed at protecting critical infrastructure. But these moves have raised questions over whether the company will scale its cybersecurity business — or even pit it against major security vendors, including partners.

Cybersecurity could match Anthropic's coding opportunity and reach $500 billion annually, Wolfe Research [analysts](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/19/anthropic-is-ready-for-its-ipo-three-analysts-see-huge-gains-for-the-stock.html) wrote in a Thursday note.

"Act 2 is Cyber which given the last week seems like it could be as big as Coding," they wrote.

That market might include AI security operations, patching or penetration testing — simulated cyberattacks utilized to identity a company's security weaknesses.

"We think that model providers will offer a solution to replace the security team that is manually analyzing data with agents who can respond at machine speed," they added.

— Samantha Subin

## Anthropic’s governance structure

Anthropic Co-Founder and CEO Dario Amodei speaks at Dreamforce 2026 summit in San Francisco, California, U.S., September 15, 2026.
Carlos Barria | Reuters

Anthropic has an [unusual governance structure](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/26/anthropic-ipo-founder-voting-control.html).

The company is a public benefit corporation, which means it's required to balance the financial interest of stockholders, the best interests of those affected by its conduct and its "company purpose." Anthropic said in its prospectus that its purpose is to facilitate the "responsible development and maintenance of advanced AI for the long-term benefit of humanity."

Anthropic's seven co-founders also wield a lot of power over the company. Prior to the completion of its offering, Anthropic issued a single share of Class F common stock to a limited liability company called "Founder LLC." Anthropic's co-founders are the sole members of Founder LLC, and the entity holds 50.1% of the voting power of the company's outstanding shares.

Each co-founder sold 3.38 million shares in the IPO, according to the prospectus, for total proceeds of $1.96 billion.

Additionally, Anthropic introduced an independent governance body called the "[Long-Term Benefit Trust](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/21/anthropic-christopher-stubbs-long-term-benefit-trust.html)" in 2023, which has the authority to elect and remove the majority of the company's board of directors. 

— Ashley Capoot

## Amodei touts Anthropic’s focus on culture and talent

Dario Amodei, co-founder and chief executive officer of Anthropic, during the company's Builder Summit in Bengaluru, India, on Monday, Feb. 16, 2026.
Samyukta Lakshmi | Bloomberg | Getty Images

Amodei told [JPMorgan Chase](/quotes/JPM/) CEO [Jamie Dimon](https://www.cnbc.com/jamie-dimon/) in a fireside chat on Friday that he thinks talent and culture are the "most important things" that drive a company's success, which is why he and his co-founders have tried to place so much emphasis on those areas at Anthropic. 

Anthropic requires every employee to pass a culture interview, which is taken very seriously, Amodei said. He added that he spends roughly half of his time focusing on talent and culture at the company. 

"We've been very disciplined about turning down people who are deeply technically talented, but who don't fit our culture," Amodei said. "We don't want people who are jerks. We don't want people who try to steal the show and don't work well with others. We don't want people who aren't oriented towards the public benefit."

Amodei also regularly holds DVQs, which stands for "Dario Vision Quests," where he speaks in front of the entire company. He said he tries to update employees by talking honestly and avoiding "corpo speak."

"It means that the leaders of the company can trust the employees," Amodei said. "The employees of the company can trust the leaders of the company to be candid. Everyone feels that we're being honest with them, and that has this enormous value."

— Ashley Capoot

## Ben Reitzes: 'The leverage here could be insane'

## Investors who flip IPO may face penalty

Retail investors with access to the Anthropic IPO who try to sell the stock for a quick profit [may face a penalty from their brokerage](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/19/anthropic-ipo-flipping-rules.html).

Anthropic made shares available to retail investors through brokerages including Charles Schwab, Fidelity, Robinhood, SoFi, E-Trade and some of their respective affiliates, the AI startup said in an IPO prospectus.

Many of them have "anti-flipping" policies meant to discourage investors who purchase IPO shares from dumping them fast.

These policies apply only to retail investors who place an order with their brokerage firm to buy the Anthropic IPO at its offering price — something many investors don't have access to.

For example, Fidelity clients who sell IPO shares within the first 15 calendar days from the start of trading in the secondary market would be barred from participating in future stock IPOs through the brokerage. Investors get a six-month ban after the first offense and could be permanently barred after the third offense.
