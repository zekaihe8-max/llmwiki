New research from Replit on scaling laws for agent swarms.

We show that scaling the number of agents can yield dramatic performance gains, but unlike traditional scaling laws, more compute doesn't necessarily translate into better results, and how agents organize themselves matters.

- Given a fixed two-hour window, Opus 5.5 went from a 3.7x speedup on NumPy with one agent to 18.9x with 32 agents.

- GPT-6.2 Astra was the strongest solo agent but exhibited negative scaling beyond 8 agents.

- Models spontaneously developed different organizational structures. Decentralized swarms scaled better than those that relied on centralized coordination.

- The parallelizability of the problem was a strong predictor of swarm performance.

- Interestingly, individual model intelligence is a poor predictor of collective intelligence.

We’re also open-sourcing Optimization-Bench to advance research into this new dimension of inference-time scaling.
