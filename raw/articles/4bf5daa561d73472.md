Coding gives you a window into what the future will look like for a large amount of knowledge work.

The first step for most jobs is that we go from doing most of the research, typing, and value creation manually (even if it’s using digital tools) to soon coordinating the work that agents are doing.

Next, as this step itself becomes redundant or slows down what agents are capable of, we will be giving agents goals to optimize against and they’ll go off on and increasingly solve larger problems for us.

This may at first blush look like something whose impact gets stuck with coding, but actually a large percentage of knowledge work may eventually just converge with this form factor due to the massive productivity gains that can be generated. Agents will use computers, leverage our existing tools or write new ones, and write code on the fly to solve larger swaths of knowledge work.

So even if a task today doesn’t seem to be “coding” directly, the task may eventually fold into being coding adjacent. Manufacturing, architecture, life sciences, material sciences, cyber, and on and on.

One big hurdle in the way ultimately will be that much of this work remains non-verifiable, so humans will be even more in the loop than they are in coding. But the part that they can hand off will just get bigger and bigger over time as the models are more capable.

So in short, pay attention to coding and you’ll get a sense for where work is going.
